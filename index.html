The system

Five parts. One closed training loop.

The system sends a cue, you strike, the target registers the hit and times it, and the result is stored. Only the targets and controller are required. Add the saber, wearables and room lighting when you want more data or more atmosphere.

  1. TGT

    Target units

    Freestanding light columns with their own processor, motion sensor, speaker and LEDs. They glow blue when it is time to strike and flash red when a hit lands. Power one on and the system finds it.

  2. MCU

    Master controller

    Chooses which target fires and when, keeps a 500 ms heartbeat on every unit, and stores each session. If a target drops out mid-session, it leaves the rotation and rejoins when it reconnects. Your session never stops.

  3. APP

    App and voice coach

    Counts you in, calls out milestones and reads your summary when the session ends. Start, stop and switch modes by voice without leaving the floor.

  4. SBR

    Instrumented saber Optional

    A light-up training blade with a motion sensor in the tip. It measures swing speed, acceleration and impact on every strike.

  5. AMB

    Ambient lighting Optional

    Wall and ceiling light units that react to the session in real time: countdowns, hits, misses and new personal bests.

Why the numbers hold up

The clock lives inside the target.

Most light-up trainers time you from a central box, so radio delay gets added to every reaction. That delay changes from strike to strike, which makes long-term comparisons unreliable.

Hiltaro targets start the clock when they light up and stop it when they feel the hit, on their own processor. The radio only carries the finished number. A 280 ms reaction this year means the same thing as a 280 ms reaction next year.

Training modes

Same hardware. New drills by software.

Each mode is a mix of cue, timing, scoring and hit detection. New modes arrive as updates, so the targets you buy today keep getting new drills.

RANDOMISED

One target lights at a random moment. Strike it and the next one fires. This is the benchmark mode for your reaction time.

PRECISION

Only one of the three light zones glows. Hit that exact zone. Trains accuracy and speed together.

AUDIO-ONLY

Build log

From one LED strip to a company.

Hiltaro started as a single sensor taped to a pipe. These entries come from the founder's own lab notes.

  1. 13 Apr 2025

    First parts on the bench

    A microcontroller, one motion sensor, a metre of addressable LEDs and lengths of PVC pipe.

  2. 14 Apr 2025

    First hit registered

    LEDs lit blue at random. A tap on the sensor turned them red.

    "When I give a little jerk to MPU6050, the LEDs turn red indicating I have hit the target. It works!!!!"
  3. 4 May 2025

    Targets go wireless

    Boards talking to each other directly over a low-latency radio link. Three separately controlled light zones per target. A transistor-driven buzzer for a hit sound loud enough for a busy floor.

  4. Jul–Aug 2025

    The body takes shape

    Translucent polycarbonate and diffused acrylic cores, layered silicone wraps, high-density LED strips for the saber, battery packs and a padded training floor.

  5. Filed

    Patent pending

    Provisional patent filed in India: "A Modular Reactive Strike Training System with Longitudinal Reaction Time Tracking".

  6. 2026

    Hiltaro Technologies Private Limited

    The company is incorporated in Karnataka to design and build sports technology and reactive training systems.

  7. Now

    Production targets and first floors

    Moving from prototypes to production units and the first training spaces.
